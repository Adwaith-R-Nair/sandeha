# Sandeha: Model Card

**Owner:** Adwaith R Nair. **Status:** main-track models are trained and measured. The public-track section is pending.

All numbers come from files in `results/`, named beside them. `docs/RESULTS.md` has the full tables and the analysis.

## In short

Five small U-Nets, trained on this project's own 2D smoke simulations, predict one step of the flow. Their mean is the prediction, and their disagreement is the doubt signal.

- **Use it for:** studying how a neural simulator and its doubt behave on 2D smoke at 128×128, and for the classroom demo.
- **Don't use it for:** any real flow, any real decision, or as a safety alarm. Doubt-based hand-off **did not beat** a fixed schedule or a random one on any test set, and on `test_b` it was worse than the fixed schedule.

**Input (6 channels, 128×128):** velocity (2), density, solid mask, inflow map and buoyancy as a constant channel.

**Output:** the change in velocity and density over one step, which is 4 reference solver steps at `dt=0.01`. Solid cells are enforced afterwards.

**Rollout:** at each step, every member starts from the shared ensemble mean. This keeps doubt from growing simply because members have drifted apart.

**Doubt:** the per-cell standard deviation across members in normalised units, reduced to one number per frame by taking the 95th percentile over fluid cells.

**Hand-off settings, frozen on validation:** threshold `tau` 0.019795792549848555, 2 solver frames per hand-off (`handoff_selection.json`). On validation this scored 0.2362 at 24.3% solver share, against 0.2566 with no hand-off.

## Training

- **Loss:** prediction error plus a divergence penalty with weight 0.1. Pushforward rollout training, with probability 0.5 and up to 3 steps, after a warm-up of 10% of the steps.
- **Optimiser:** AdamW, learning rate 3e-4, weight decay 0.01, batch size 16, mixed precision.
- **Budget:** 48 minutes per member on an RTX 3060 Laptop GPU. Best checkpoints were at steps 29,000 to 31,254, chosen on validation rollout error (`ensemble.json`).

## Measured performance

Frames 21 to 70, starting from the stored reference at frame 20. Lower score is better. No rollout blew up on any set (`exp_accuracy.json`, `exp_doubt.json`).

| Set | One member | Ensemble | Coarse solver | Solver-only run | Ensemble ms per frame | Doubt, within-time Spearman |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `test_a` | 0.292 | 0.256 [0.220, 0.292] | 0.346 | 0.097 | 15.2 | 0.671 [0.455, 0.796] |
| `test_b` | 0.358 | 0.308 [0.272, 0.345] | 0.369 | 0.098 | 14.9 | 0.522 [0.286, 0.673] |
| `test_c` low | 0.343 | 0.219 [0.194, 0.240] | 0.152 | 0.018 | 14.9 | 0.403 [0.001, 0.709] |
| `test_c` high | 0.561 | 0.526 [0.495, 0.563] | 0.516 | 0.278 | 14.9 | 0.388 [0.042, 0.633] |

**Speed.** The reference solver took 262.8, 192.4 and 194.4 ms per frame on `test_a`, `test_b` and `test_c`, so the ensemble is about 13 to 17 times faster with all 5 members counted. The reference is this project's own solver and wasn't tuned for speed. In the demo's AI mode the ensemble ran at 61.3 frames per second (`demo_bench.json`).

**Hand-off, at equal solver budget inside the window (`exp_handoff.json`).**

## Where not to trust it

| Situation | What was measured | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Low doubt on an unfamiliar flow** | On the low side of `test_c`, doubt is lower than on `test_a` (chance of higher doubt 0.103), and no frame passes `tau`. Yet the ensemble is less accurate than the coarse solver there, 0.219 against 0.152, and makes 23% too much smoke | Don't read low doubt as "safe". This is the most serious failure in this card |
| **Unseen shapes** | Doubt doesn't rise on `test_b` (chance of higher doubt 0.479 [0.335, 0.615]), though error does | Don't use doubt as a detector of unfamiliar geometry |
| **Fast, strongly driven flows** | On the high side of `test_c`, score is 0.526, usable frames 81.0, and the coarse solver is no worse. 92.6% of frames are over `tau`, so the system takes 257.7 ms per frame, slower than the reference's 194.4 ms | Doubt is over the threshold almost all the time here, so it says little about any one frame. Don't treat the hybrid as a speed-up in this range |
| **Long rollouts** | Evidence is for frames 21 to 70. Over 100 frames, the coarse solver's mean error is below the ensemble's on `test_b`, 0.503 against 0.529, with overlapping intervals. The within-time Spearman on `test_b` falls to 0.271 over frames 21 to 120 | Treat later frames as less reliable. Nothing was measured past frame 120, and the demo can run longer than that |
| **Doubt as a moment-by-moment alarm** | Post-hoc and exploratory: after removing time, the share of log-doubt variance that is between scenes is about 90%, and 83% on the low side of `test_c`, and scene doubt tracks largest flow travel with Spearman 0.83 to 0.86 on `test_a`, `test_b` and the high side of `test_c`, though not on the low side | It's closer to a label on the scene than a warning about the current frame |
| **Single-signal choice** | A plain divergence signal with no ensemble correlated better than doubt on `test_b`, 0.676 against 0.522, and worse on the high side of `test_c`, 0.174 against 0.388 | Don't assume the ensemble's doubt is the best available signal |
| **Pointwise accuracy after about `t=1`** | The reference is one realisation of a chaotic flow (`PREREGISTRATION.md`) | Read the error with the secondary measures. It isn't a claim of physical wrongness or rightness |
| **Fine scales** | Hand-offs lose more of the finest scales than the ensemble does, on `test_b` -0.223 for doubt and -0.269 for fixed against -0.208 in the highest spectrum band | Don't expect a brief solver hand-off to restore fine detail |

## Not measured

- **Drawing in the demo.** People can draw any shape at any moment. `test_a` covers late circles, rectangles and walls, and the post-hoc work didn't separate their effect. `test_b` and `test_c` have no late obstacles. So a hand-drawn shape added while the demo runs is outside what was measured.
- **Starting from anything but a stored reference at frame 20.** Every measured rollout starts there.
- **Any grid but 128×128, any domain but this closed box, anything in 3D.** The models aren't designed for these.
- **Real-world flows.** There's no comparison with measured smoke at all.
- **Other seeds of the whole pipeline.** The dataset was generated once, and each test set has 30 scenes or fewer. The low side of `test_c` has only 10.
- **Whether the solver tests pass on the last commit.** See `docs/RESULTS.md`. They're to be rerun when the GPU is free.

## Reduced trainings

Each was one seed with a shorter, 25-minute training, for comparison only. Without the divergence penalty or without pushforward, scores were worse than the full member by 0.033 to 0.097 (`exp_ablation.json`). The shorter training is part of that difference. They aren't part of the ensemble.
